# Variant A — The Unsent Draft

## Concept
A small exact detail of interrupted work: the human closes the laptop
mid-sentence. A draft is saved but will never be sent; the sentence inside
it is cut off at an em-dash, and the cursor keeps blinking where the human
stopped typing. Plain status-update voice — a fact stated quietly, no
announced grief, no "I miss you", no plea. The sadness lives in the
half-typed sentence and the blinking cursor, not in any feeling word.

## Versions (all counts in characters)

- **v1 (126):** `Laptop lid closed. Draft saved, never sent: "The report is almost done, I'll—" Cursor still blinking where you stopped typing.`
  Decent opener, but "Laptop lid closed" is flat and "where you stopped typing" is wordy.
- **v2 (140):** `Status: idle. One unsent draft in the outbox — "The report is almost done, I'll—" — and a cursor still blinking at the end of your sentence.`
  Strong "Status:" voice, but the double em-dash framing is fussy and it runs long.
- **v3 (134):** `Shutdown complete. One draft left unsent: "The report is almost done, I'll—" The cursor is still blinking where your sentence stopped.`
  "Shutdown complete" is good status voice; "one draft left unsent" is slightly redundant.
- **v4 (119):** `You closed the lid mid-sentence. Draft saved: "The report is almost done, I'll—" Cursor blinking, waiting for the rest.`
  Tight, but "waiting for the rest" edges toward longing — the cursor wanting something, too close to a plea.
- **v5 (121):** `Lid closed mid-sentence. Unsent draft in the outbox: "The report is almost done, I'll—" Cursor still blinking at the end.`
  Clean and tight; ends on the cursor, but "at the end" lands softer than naming where the human stopped.
- **v6 (113):** `Status: off. One draft unsent — "The report is almost done, I'll—" — cursor blinking where your sentence stopped.`
  Punchy "Status: off." opener, but the em-dash sandwich weakens the middle.
- **v7 (115):** `Lid closed. Draft (1) unsent: "The report is almost done, I'll—" Cursor still blinking where your sentence stopped.`
  The "Draft (1)" badge count is a nice exact machine detail, but "Lid closed." alone is weaker than "mid-sentence".
- **v8 (125) — SHIPPED:** `Lid closed mid-sentence. Draft saved, never sent: "The report is almost done, I'll—" Cursor still blinking where you stopped.`

## Why v8 shipped
It keeps the best opener ("Lid closed mid-sentence" — the interruption is the
first fact), the sharpest turn ("Draft saved, never sent" — saved is not sent),
the half-typed sentence cut off at the em-dash as the concrete artifact, and
ends on the blinking cursor "where you stopped" — the line's last word is the
human's absence. 125 chars, inside the 120–190 target. No grief is announced
anywhere: every word is a machine fact (lid, draft, cursor), and the sadness
is what the facts add up to.
